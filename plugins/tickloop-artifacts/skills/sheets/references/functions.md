- These are all the functions there are so far. A name that is not among them, such as `SPLIT`, `JOIN`, `ARRAYFORMULA`,
  `REGEXMATCH`, `COUNTUNIQUE`, `SORTN` or `AGGREGATE`, comes to `#NAME?`: use `TEXTBEFORE`, `TEXTAFTER` or `MID` with
  `FIND` for `SPLIT`, `TEXTJOIN` for `JOIN`, `REGEXTEST` for `REGEXMATCH`, `COUNTA(UNIQUE(..))` for `COUNTUNIQUE` and
  `TAKE` of a `SORT` for `SORTN`. `ARRAYFORMULA` is not needed: a formula alone in a cell spills its list (below).
  Capitals do not count in a name: `=sum(B2:B3)` works as `=SUM(B2:B3)` does.
- Beside them a formula takes `+ - * / ^ & %`, `= <> < <= > >=`, brackets, numbers, `"text"`, and `B3`, `$B$3`, `B3:D4`,
  `Notes!B3`, `'My sheet'!B3`. There are no named ranges: `=SUM(Sales)` is `#NAME?`.
- ARRAYS IN BRACES: `{1,2;3,4}` is a list of two rows of two values. It holds numbers, `"text"`, `TRUE`, `FALSE` and
  errors, not cells: join ranges with `HSTACK` and `VSTACK`. A function that reads a range reads it,
  `=VLOOKUP(B2,{0,"low";5,"high"},2)`. Some functions do not take braces here: give `LINEST`, `LOGEST`, `TREND` and
  `GROWTH` cell ranges, and count several values with one `COUNTIF` each, `=COUNTIF(B2:B9,"a")+COUNTIF(B2:B9,"b")`,
  not with a list of criteria.
- A FORMULA IS WORKED OUT THE SAME WAY IN EVERY LOCALE, and you write it the US way (the Skill's "What a cell holds").
  Keep numbers outside quotation marks, `">"&1.25`, and dates as `DATE(2026,3,2)` or `"2026-03-02"`, and a formula is
  right whatever the locale. Compare `MONTH(B2)`, not a month's name from `TEXT(B2,"mmmm")`.
- WHOLE COLUMNS AND ROWS: `B:B`, `B:D`, `2:2` and `Notes!B:B` reach to the sheet's last row or column, and cover rows
  and columns added later. A range with no end, `B2:B`, is `#VALUE!` here: name the whole column, or both corners. So
  total a column that grows with `=SUM(B:B)` from a cell outside it, under a header in words, or with a range that both
  corners name, which grows as rows are inserted among its own. A formula inside what it reads is `#CIRC!`: keep it out
  of those columns or rows. One that names a row or column which the sheet lacks, or a sheet that is not there, is an
  error. `ROWS(B:B)` is the sheet's row count.
- ARRAY MATH: an operator over several cells works cell by cell and comes to a list: `=SUMPRODUCT((A2:A9="x")*B2:B9)`,
  `=SUM(B2:B9*C2:C9)`. A comparison gives TRUE or FALSE, which a sum passes over, so `=SUM(A2:A9="x")` is 0: multiply
  as above, or use `COUNTIFS` or `SUMIFS`, which cost far less. Sizes that do not fit, `A1:A3+B1:B5`, are an error.
  `AND` and `OR` give one answer for all their cells: for each row, multiply the conditions for "and", and for "or" write
  `((B2:B9>5)+(C2:C9="x"))>0`. Alone in a cell, a list spills (below).
- `SUBTOTAL` takes the codes 1 to 11 and 101 to 111, and references only. A grand total is `=SUM(B5,B9)` over the
  subtotals.
- A name in `LET` that spells an address, as `q4` does, is an error: name it `q_4` or `qFour`.
- A formula holds 8,192 bytes (`formulaTooLong`). No write is refused for anything else that its formula says: what is
  wrong shows as the formula's `value`, `#NAME?` for an unknown function, `#ERROR!` or another error for text that
  cannot be read.
- SPILLING: `=UNIQUE(A2:A9)` in D2 comes to a list. D2 shows its first value and the blank cells under it the rest, so
  leave room. A read gives D2 a `spill`, `{"kind":"fills","to":"D6"}`, and each other cell of the list as
  `{"spilledFrom":"D2","value":…}`. All of it shows or none: if anything is in the way, a filled cell or a merged one,
  D2 is `#SPILL!`. Clear that cell or move the formula. A list keeps only as many rows and columns as it fills, so it
  grows and shrinks with what it comes to. No two lists may land on one cell: where they would, one of them is
  `#SPILL!`, so put lists side by side, not one under another. A list that would run past the sheet's last row or
  column is `#SPILL!` too: insert rows or columns there ([rows and columns](rows-and-columns.md)).
- Other formulas read spilled values as any cell's: `=COUNTA(D2:D9)`. Inside a function that reads a range nothing
  spills: `=COUNTA(UNIQUE(A2:A9))` is one number.
- `SORT` lists a range's rows by one of its columns: `=SORT(A2:C9,2,-1)` goes by the second, largest first. The order is
  1 or `TRUE` for ascending, -1 or `FALSE` for descending. `=TAKE(SORT(A2:C9,2,-1),3)` is its first 3 rows. `SORTBY`
  takes several keys. `TRANSPOSE` turns its range: `=TRANSPOSE(A2:A9)` fills eight columns.
- `VSTACK` and `HSTACK` keep room for all their ranges together, which can be on any sheets. The `#N/A` beside a smaller
  range is a value of the list, which `=IFERROR(VSTACK(..),"")` clears.
- REGULAR EXPRESSIONS: `=REGEXTEST(A2,"^[A-Z]{3}-[0-9]+$")` is `TRUE` if any part of the text matches, so `^` and `$`
  ask for all of it. `=REGEXEXTRACT(A2,"[0-9]+")` is the first match, as a text, and `#N/A` if nothing matches.
  `=REGEXREPLACE(A2,"(\w+) (\w+)","$2 $1")` replaces every match: `$1` is the first group and `$0` the whole match.
  Capitals count unless the pattern starts with `(?i)`. Give them text: `A2&""` or `TEXT(A2,"yyyy-mm-dd")` for a number
  or a date.
- `=FILTER(A2:B9,B2:B9>5)` lists the rows of A2:B9 for which the condition is `TRUE`. The condition is a column as tall
  as the range. For two conditions multiply them, `(B2:B9>5)*(C2:C9="x")`. As the condition, `A2:A9<>""` drops the rows
  whose A is blank. A value in the last place is the answer if no row is kept, and without one that is `#CALC!`.
- `MMULT`, `MINVERSE`, `MDETERM`, `LINEST`, `LOGEST`, `TREND` and `GROWTH` need a number in every cell, so no whole
  columns.
- `RAND` and `RANDBETWEEN` draw new numbers whenever the Sheets are worked out again, after anyone's edit, so what you
  read is not what the person will see for long. Numbers that must stay are written as values.
- `NOW` and `TODAY` tell the time in the Sheets' time zone.
- THE TIME ZONE is the whole Sheets', for everyone. `readSheets` gives it as `"timeZone"`. While there is none, the time
  is UTC. `{"kind":"setTimeZone","timeZone":"Asia/Kolkata"}`, the first edit of its `edit_sheet`, sets it, and `null`
  takes it away. It takes `UTC` or a city's name as the IANA database has it now: `EST`, `GMT+5` and `US/Eastern` are
  refused. Set it when asked, or first in Sheets that you just made, if you know where the person is. Never guess, and
  never replace one unasked.
- THE LOCALE is the whole Sheets' too: `"locale"` in `readSheets`. `{"kind":"setLocale","locale":"de-DE"}` sets it, one
  of `en-US`, `en-GB`, `de-DE`, `es-ES`, `fr-FR` and `it-IT` (any other is refused with `localeNotKnown`). It rewrites
  no cell, but each `shown` changes; a formula's answer does not. Set it
  when asked, or first in Sheets that you just made, if they are in `en-US` and you know where the person is. Never
  guess.
- WHAT A FORMULA'S CELL SHOWS: `DATE`, `EDATE`, `EOMONTH`, `WORKDAY` and `TODAY` show a date, and `NOW` a date and a
  time. Other answers may show as plain numbers, as `TIME`, twice a percent or 50% of 200 can, or a difference of two
  dates as a date. So give a formula's cell the number format that you mean with a `formatCells` in the same update
  ([formats](formats.md)). `readCells` goes by what the cell shows: it gives such a date or time as `{"date"}`,
  `{"time"}` or `{"dateTime"}`, and such money or percent as `{"number","shown"}`.
